Is padre.gg Safe? Lookalikes and Fake Apps

6 min read·Padre Terminal Blog

"Is padre.gg safe" is one of the most common questions first-time users type into a search bar before connecting a wallet — and it's a reasonable one to ask in a space where lookalike sites and fake apps are common. Here's what's worth checking.

There is no official mobile app

Padre Terminal — now branded Terminal — does not have a confirmed official app in the Apple App Store or Google Play Store. It runs as a progressive web app (PWA), installed directly from the browser to your home screen. Independent reviewers who specifically checked app stores for an official listing came back empty.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOU

Any app calling itself "Padre Terminal" or "Padre Trading" in an app store deserves a hard look before you install it or connect a wallet to it. When in doubt, install the PWA directly from the browser at the verified URL instead.

The domain is trade.padre.gg

The verified trading URL is trade.padre.gg. A number of other domains using "padre" or "terminal" in the name exist online — some are legitimate affiliate or informational pages (like this one, which is disclosed below), and some are unrelated sites simply using similar branding to attract search traffic. Neither category is automatically dangerous, but none of them are the platform itself.

Before connecting a wallet anywhere, the basic check is the same one that applies to every on-chain platform: confirm you're on the domain you intended to be on, and bookmark it once verified rather than re-searching each time.

What "safe" actually depends on here

  • Self-custody, not the platform, is your main safeguard. Since Padre Terminal never takes custody of your funds, the platform itself can't run off with your assets the way a custodial exchange theoretically could — your risk is concentrated in wallet security, not platform trust in the traditional sense.
  • Seed phrase requests are the clearest red flag. A legitimate wallet connection never requires typing your seed phrase into a website. If any page — official-looking or not — asks for it, that's the signal to stop.
  • Reviews vary in reliability. Independent reviewers who explicitly fact-check claims (noting when something is "not publicly confirmed" rather than assuming) are generally more trustworthy than pages that only list features without caveats.
